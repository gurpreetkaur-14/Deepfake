Facial Texture Anomalies

GAN and autoencoder decoders frequently produce oversmoothed skin tones or irregular micro-pore repetitions that differ from optical camera sensors.

Focus: Skin pore gradient & FFT frequency domain

Boundary Blending Artifacts

Poisson image blending and convex hull warping create subtle boundary edge mismatches where the generated face is spliced onto the source frame.

Focus: Jawline, hairline, and neck transition contours

Lighting & Specular Reflection

Corneal reflections and directional shadows often mismatch ambient lighting vectors in the background setting, exposing synthetic composite generation.

Focus: Corneal specular highlight vectors

Facial Action Units (AUs)

Divergence between eye blink rates, pupil asymmetry, and uncoordinated muscle tension across the nasolabial fold provide structural cues.

Focus: Landmark distance ratios & landmark jitter

Temporal Frame-to-Frame Coherence (Video Mode)

While single frames may appear visually convincing, frame-to-frame synthesis frequently produces flickering landmarks, jittering skin margins, and motion vector discontinuities across sequential video clips.

Evaluation Metric: Inter-frame SSIM & Optical Flow Discrepancy
COMPARATIVE FORENSICS

What Does Our AI Look For?

Understanding the biometric and physical divergence between organic camera captures and neural network generators.

Real Media

Authentic Optical Capture
Pristine
  • Natural Facial Texture: Realistic pores, micro-wrinkles, natural skin specularities, and consistent focal depth.
  • Consistent Lighting: Uniform illumination angles, realistic shadows on nostrils, collarbones, and ambient reflections.
  • Seamless Boundaries: No blurring or color gradient shift where facial edges meet the neck, ears, and hair strands.
  • Physiological Synchronization: Normal eye blinking cadence, pupil dilation responsiveness, and symmetrical iris circularity.
  • Stable Video Motion: Smooth optical flow across continuous frames without flickering pixels or sudden morphing.

Manipulated Media

Synthetic / Deepfake Generation
Anomalous
  • Texture Inconsistencies: Unnatural plastic-like skin blurring, missing facial hair fidelity, or recurrent checkerboard artifacts.
  • Lighting Anomalies: Highlights on cheeks that contradict the primary ambient light source visible in the background.
  • Facial Blending Boundaries: Subtle ghosting rings, color mismatch, or blurred borders around jawlines and foreheads.
  • Unusual Facial Patterns: Irregular teeth alignment, deformed earrings or eyeglasses, and unnatural pupil reflections.
  • Temporal Jitter: Inter-frame micro-warping, sudden morphs during head rotations, and frame-rate phase mismatches.
METHODOLOGY WORKFLOW

How DeepGuard Works

DeepGuard processes uploaded media through a sequential computer-vision and deep-learning pipeline before generating an explainable prediction.

01

Upload Media

Ingestion of image (JPEG/PNG) or video (MP4/AVI) container. Verification of payload integrity and codec decoding.

02

Face Detection

OpenCV Haar Cascades / MTCNN locate region of interest (ROI) and extract 68-point facial landmarks for geometric alignment.

03

Preprocessing

Cropped facial frames are standardized to 224×224 pixels, normalized to [-1, 1] range, and batched for network ingestion.

04

Feature Extraction

Convolutional kernels distill deep spatial hierarchies, high-frequency residuals, and texture boundary feature maps.

05

Deep Learning Model

MobileNetV2 inverted residual blocks with linear bottlenecks perform lightweight, low-latency binary classification.

06

Prediction

Softmax output generates class probabilities, categorizing the specimen definitively as either Pristine Real or Deepfake.

07

Confidence & Explainability Output

The platform synthesizes the confidence score, highlights anomalous face regions via Grad-CAM attribution heatmaps, and renders a verifiable diagnostic dossier for human verification.

Educational Overview

What Is a Deepfake?

Deepfakes are digitally manipulated or AI-generated media created using deep generative models (such as GANs, Diffusion Models, and Variational Autoencoders) that make individuals appear to utter words or perform actions they never conducted in reality.

Why Deepfake Detection Matters

Synthetic media presents profound challenges to societal trust, information ecosystems, and personal security.

Disinformation & Fake News

Fabricated speeches and crisis simulations can trigger public alarm, disrupt electoral integrity, and destabilize diplomatic communications.

Identity Theft & Impersonation

Unauthorized cloning of faces and likenesses allows malicious entities to forge identity documents and bypass basic liveness checks.

Financial & Corporate Fraud

Synthetic video calls imitating corporate executives have already orchestrated multi-million dollar unauthorized bank transfers.

Targeted Harassment & Defamation

Non-consensual deepfake imagery targeting individuals inflicts severe emotional distress and irreversible professional reputational damage.

The "Liar's Dividend"

As fake media proliferates, bad actors can conveniently dismiss genuine recordings of corruption or wrongdoing as mere "AI fabrications."

Judicial & Forensics Integrity

Courts and journalism organizations require verified, explainable algorithmic audit trails to admit audiovisual exhibits into public record.

Privacy & Responsible AI Principles
